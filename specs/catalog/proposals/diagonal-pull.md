# Diagonal-pull resolution

Status: active as one bounded, source-exact cable fixture. The old commercial
lever-high-row candidate remains excluded; it did not become the family
definition.

## Resolution

`diagonal-pull` is now an independent family because Lorenzetti et al. (2017)
directly measured a seated **45-degree lat pulldown** rather than merely naming
an oblique-looking machine. Fifteen healthy adults performed two sets of eight
repetitions on a Technogym Ercolina Rehab adjustable cable station at 10% and
25% of bodyweight. The study used 22-camera three-dimensional motion capture,
an inline load cell, and markers along the cable to resolve its force
direction.

The exact fixture is:

- seated on a flat bench with the feet on the floor and no thigh restraint;
- bilateral wider-than-shoulder-width pronated grip on a straight bar;
- elbows extended with the arms on the source-prescribed 45-degree start line;
- bar pulled to chest contact;
- head kept straight and core engaged; and
- slow return to the same start.

The 45-degree trial produced the largest sagittal shoulder range among the
paper's lat pulldown, 45-degree pulldown, seated row, and upright row trials.
That directly supports a primarily sagittal shoulder-extension pull whose
athlete-relative start and chest endpoint are neither the overhead start of
`vertical-pull` nor the near-horizontal corridor of
`shoulder-extension-row`.

The source does not define its 45-degree instruction in an anatomical
coordinate system. The contract therefore preserves
`sourcePrescribedFortyFiveDegrees` rather than converting it into a published
humeral angle or a generic numeric direction band. This is an exact-fixture
classification, not permission to infer diagonal ownership from room-space
cable angle, a product name, or an uncalibrated photograph.

## Canonical action boundary

The active signature is deliberately narrower than the old lever-high-row
draft:

- conditioned `shoulder.extension` from the flexed start;
- `elbow.flexion`; and
- sagittal plane basis from shoulder extension.

No shoulder-adduction action is authored. Lorenzetti reports the 45-degree
trial as primarily sagittal, while the traditional vertical lat pulldown was
the only compared exercise producing the paper's large frontal-plane moment.
No scapular prime action is authored either: the study did not construct a
scapular coordinate system. The scapula remains a stability demand, with
middle/lower trapezius and rhomboids credited only as stabilizers.

The contract forbids every other catalog action as a deliberate prime. This
does not claim that a living shoulder blade or spine is motionless. It prevents
future records from manufacturing a second geometry by adding adduction,
retraction, rotation, trunk extension, or lower-body drive without a separate
family review.

## Role policy

Lorenzetti measured kinematics and kinetics, not EMG. The categorical roster
is therefore an explicit mechanics triangulation:

- `lats` primary for the reviewed loaded shoulder-extension corridor;
- `teresMajor` and `deltoidPosterior` secondary for shoulder extension;
- `bicepsBrachii`, `brachialis`, and `brachioradialis` secondary for elbow
  flexion;
- `trapeziusMiddle`, `trapeziusLower`, and `rhomboids` stabilizers only because
  no dynamic scapular action was measured;
- `externalRotators` for shoulder control;
- `fingerFlexors` plus `extensorCarpiRadialis` for the static bar and wrist
  task; and
- `abs`, `obliques`, and `lumbarExtensors` as trunk/pelvis stabilizers.

Ackland and Holzbaur support the shoulder capabilities. Lehman's seated-row
EMG is component context for latissimus, elbow-flexor, and combined
middle-trapezius/rhomboid participation, not an exact-path role oracle. No
cross-channel EMG ranking or commercial target-muscle claim is used.

## Runtime record

The only active record is:

- `seated-45-degree-cable-pulldown` — **Seated 45-Degree Cable Pulldown**
  (`45-Degree Lat Pulldown`, `Diagonal Cable Pulldown`).

It is `compound / pull / diagonal`, sagittal, cable, bilateral, externally
loaded, dynamic strength, and rep tracked. The reviewed dose is eight reps at
10% or 25% of bodyweight. The runtime cannot derive a live cable-stack seed
from body mass, so 35 lb / 15 kg is a disclosed representative product seed;
the movement definition tells the athlete to enter the actual chosen load.

The source measured spinal behavior and instructed a straight head/back with
core engagement. It did not prove a position-held spine. The contract encodes
`spineMotion: nonstandardized` and requires trunk stabilizers rather than
claiming isolation.

## Excluded boundaries

| Candidate | Owner or reason |
|---|---|
| Overhead cable, lever, assisted, or bodyweight pulldown/pull-up | `vertical-pull`; overhead start and shoulder-adduction component. |
| Near-horizontal tucked or scapular-path row | `shoulder-extension-row`. |
| Flared rear-delt high row | `shoulder-horizontal-abduction-row`. |
| Straight-arm pulldown | `shoulder-extension-isolation`; no elbow flexion. |
| Hammer/other commercial lever “high row” | Still excluded. Public product material does not establish equivalence to the measured 45-degree cable fixture. |
| Landmine, D.Y., kneeling, unilateral, rotational, or self-anchored diagonal pull | Different support, path, or action topology; separately review before activation. |
| Any pull with deliberate trunk or lower-body propulsion | Violates the strict family boundary. |

Bare `High Row` is intentionally not an alias anywhere because it has
incompatible meanings across research and commercial equipment. The archived
[lever-high-row measurement protocol](diagonal-pull-measurement-protocol.md)
remains useful only if a future model-specific lever record is proposed.
Passing that protocol would not automatically prove equivalence to this
family.

## Activation gates

The integration suite must pin all of the following:

1. exact single-record identity, aliases, classification, loads, and evidence;
2. exact two-action signature and full 42-action forbidden complement;
3. every fixed classification value, axis, domain, and exercise value;
4. removal or demotion of every required mover and stabilizer;
5. sole ownership of `direction: diagonal` among pull families;
6. absence of shoulder adduction, scapular retraction, axial rotation, and
   trunk/lower-body prime actions;
7. runtime projection of `familyID`, sagittal plane, diagonal direction,
   external load, and categorical roles;
8. analytics treatment as a diagonal pull: it belongs to broad push/pull
   reporting but does not silently enter the horizontal or vertical symmetry
   rows; and
9. roadmap closure with generic grip as the only remaining unresolved work
   item.
