## Evidence and load-history gate

The VECTOR manual directly defines elbow extension and, together with its paired
technical figures, supports a disclosed categorical shoulder-extension
inference. That exact selectorized record stays separate from suspended or
assisted dips and from upper-arm-held elbow isolation. The Hammer PL-PO manual
defines its seated, belted, elbow-pad-plus-handle topology; the active record
logs total added plate mass across that fixture and excludes starting
resistance.

The Panatta catalogue defines two independent 45-degree platforms, a reciprocal
footboard-coupling lock, independent stops, and separate starting resistance.
The single-leg record disengages the reciprocal coupling, retains the unused
side on its independent safety, requires equal plates on both working sides,
and records that per-side value once. The MTSCP and MTSSP product standards
define separate stacks and independent arms; both sides must use the same
stack setting, which is entered once rather than summed. No machine value is
comparable across fixtures.

## Belt Squat remains a separate blocked family

Joseph et al. (2020), DOI
[`10.2478/hukin-2019-0126`](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2019-0126), studied a
Wenning Strength Belt Squat Machine, fourth generation. It directly supports
the pivoting-tongue fixture, ankle-to-attachment alignment, tongue parallel to
the floor at standing, preferred taped stance, two-second phases, and a
hands-not-assisting rule. It also found materially lower trunk and gluteal EMG
than parallel back squats while measured quadriceps differences were not
significant. The study performed no kinematic comparison, did not specify a
reproducible depth or confirm full hip extension, and does not define practical
plate-mass equivalence through the pivot.

The current Hammer PL-BSQ is not a substitute: it adds four belt anchors,
front and rear loading points at different pivot distances, optional band
resistance, and conflicting published starting-resistance values. Activation
requires either an authoritative Wenning Gen4 manual that fixes ROM, belt and
tongue geometry, resistance ratio, and logged-load meaning, or separately
matched Hammer biomechanics plus one canonical anchor and loading convention.
Until then, no Belt Squat alias or runtime record is emitted and ordinary squat
history remains untouched.
