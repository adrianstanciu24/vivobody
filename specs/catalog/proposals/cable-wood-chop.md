# Cable wood chop fixture decision

Status: blocked by fixture ambiguity; not active catalog input.

## Requested exercise

The requested fixture is a standing, high-to-low, two-hand cable movement from
above one shoulder toward the opposite hip, repeated in both directions, with
the feet fixed while the trunk flexes and rotates. The remaining ambiguity is
whether the pelvis rotates under those planted feet and whether the arms move
relative to the torso or only transmit the trunk-driven path.

## Boundary decision

No active family owns the unresolved name. `spine-rotation` excludes wood chops
and combined flexion-rotation; `spine-flexion` excludes rotation and lower-body
motion; `anti-rotation` owns a held trunk rather than a moving chop. A supported
fixture would require a narrow new family rather than weakening any of those
contracts.

The unresolved geometry is load-bearing:

- The ACE high-to-low chop pivots the feet, shifts weight, and rotates the hips
  and torso while the handle travels toward the opposite hip.
- Published cable wood-chop research also includes low-to-high diagonal pulls
  performed while keeping the torso as still as possible.
- Within the requested planted-foot flexion-and-rotation fixture, a pelvis-held
  trunk-only interpretation and a pelvis-rotating version with active arm travel
  still declare different prime actions, stability demands, muscle roles, and
  neighboring-family exclusions.

## Evidence reviewed on 2026-09-19

- American Council on Exercise, *Chops*, official technical standard:
  <https://www.acefitness.org/cp/pdfs/CertifiedNews/AugSept09Cert.pdf>. It
  establishes the high cable, single handle, two-hand grip, above-shoulder to
  opposite-hip path, controlled return, mirrored directions, foot pivot, weight
  shift, and hip-and-torso rotation. It does not isolate spinal flexion or
  establish a categorical muscle hierarchy.
- Zemková et al., *A Novel Method for Assessing Muscle Power During the Standing
  Cable Wood Chop Exercise*, DOI
  <https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000001692>, PMID 27806016. It establishes
  a standing weight-stack wood-chop task and repeated external-load performance,
  but the accessible report is not precise enough to resolve the requested
  segment contributions.
- Vasudevan et al., *Comparison of Muscle Onset Activation Sequences Between a
  Golf or Tennis Swing and Common Training Exercises Using Surface
  Electromyography*, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1155/2016/3987486>, PMCID
  PMC4925984. Its diagonal cable pull uses a low-to-high example while asking the
  athlete to keep the torso as still as possible, so it is a conflicting fixture
  rather than support for a high-to-low trunk-driven chop.

## Rejected inferences

- A diagonal handle path alone does not prove dynamic spinal flexion.
- The name does not establish whether the shoulders pull the handle relative to
  the torso, whether the pelvis rotates, or whether the knees and hips propel the
  repetition.
- Oblique activity in other rotation exercises does not define an exact role
  hierarchy for every exercise called a wood chop.
- A selected cable-stack value is not comparable across unlike machines.

## Concrete unlock

Keep the feet planted and the high-to-low trunk flexion-and-rotation path, then
resolve these two remaining axes:

1. whether the pelvis stays held or rotates beneath the moving trunk; and
2. whether the arms stay fixed relative to the torso or actively move the cable
   through the diagonal path.

Then review that fixture's exact prime actions and muscle roles before adding an
active family. Low-to-high, horizontal, kneeling, anti-motion, landmine,
medicine-ball, and hip-driven variants remain separate.
