# Batch 1 — shoulder-action boundary review

Status: integrated review record. Seven narrow families are approved for
activation; two candidates remain evidence-gated proposals.

## Outcome

| Candidate | Decision | Load-bearing boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `shoulder-extension-isolation` | Activate narrowly | Shoulder extension with no elbow flexion, scapular retraction, or body propulsion |
| `chest-fly` | Activate narrowly | Shoulder horizontal adduction with the elbow angle held, not elbow extension |
| `reverse-fly` | Activate narrowly | Shoulder horizontal abduction with the elbow angle held; scapular retraction is not silently inferred |
| `shoulder-flexion-raise` | Activate narrowly | Sagittal humeral elevation with the elbow angle held and no pressing action |
| `shoulder-abduction-raise` | Activate narrowly | Frontal humeral elevation with the elbow angle held and no upright-row elbow flexion |
| `shoulder-external-rotation` | Activate narrowly | Isolated glenohumeral external rotation, not a reverse fly, face pull, or Cuban press |
| `shoulder-internal-rotation` | Activate narrowly | Isolated glenohumeral internal rotation, not a fly, pullover, or diagnostic test |
| `scapular-retraction` | Defer | Direct loaded scapular motion and a reproducible non-row fixture are not yet established |
| `upright-row` | Defer | Direct loaded kinematics do not yet establish its scapular action and humeral path |

Activation does not mean that every familiar exercise name is admitted. Each
contract starts with the smallest roster directly supported by a reviewed
setup. Incline or decline flies, fly presses, face pulls, Cuban presses,
scaption raises, raises above shoulder height, high pulls, pullovers with
material elbow excursion, and diagnostic shoulder tests remain outside the
initial contracts.

## Shared movement vocabulary

`elbowMotion` describes whether a meaningful elbow-joint excursion occurs;
`angleHeld` permits a deliberately maintained soft bend. It is independent of
`elbowPosture`, which describes the held position where a family needs that
distinction.

`humeralRotation` describes glenohumeral rotation and must not be inferred from
`gripOrientation`. A pronated hand can coexist with a neutral humeral rotation,
and a grip label cannot add an internal- or external-rotation prime action.

`elevationPath` is torso-relative and this batch admits only the reviewed
cardinal paths `sagittal|frontal`. A scapular-plane path is not pre-approved;
scaption remains deferred until its own reviewed fixture and boundary decision.
`humerothoracicStartElevationDegrees` and
`humerothoracicEndElevationDegrees` describe arm elevation relative to the
thorax; they are authoring conventions, not isolated glenohumeral angles or
laboratory precision claims.

Every activated Batch-1 family fixes `lowerBodyContribution` to `none` and
explicitly forbids spinal motion, hip extension, knee extension, and ankle
plantarflexion as prime actions. Standing fixtures may demand trunk
stabilization, but neither trunk nor leg motion is allowed to create the
repetition.

Scapular translation and orientation are separate concepts. Reviewed
elevation kinematics can support upward rotation and posterior tilt without
proving protraction, retraction, or a fixed amount of either orientation.
Support contact also does not automatically pin the posterior scapulae.

## Boundary matrix

- Fly versus press: both can use horizontal shoulder motion, but only a press
  has dynamic elbow extension. A fly/press hybrid requires a separate review.
- Reverse fly versus shoulder-height row: both can horizontally abduct the
  shoulder, but the row requires elbow flexion and reviewed scapular
  retraction; the reverse fly holds the elbow angle and does not inherit
  retraction from the row.
- Reverse fly versus scapular retraction: humeral motion owns the reverse fly;
  a future retraction family must hold shoulder and elbow angles while the
  scapulae translate.
- Front raise versus press: a raise holds the elbow angle; vertical pressing
  extends it. An incline press also combines shoulder components that a strict
  sagittal raise forbids.
- Lateral raise versus upright row: both candidates abduct the shoulder, but
  only the upright row dynamically flexes the elbow. The unresolved upright-row
  scapular signature cannot be copied into the lateral raise.
- Straight-arm extension versus pull: the isolation family holds the elbow
  angle and forbids scapular retraction; vertical pulls and rows require elbow
  flexion and their own scapular actions.
- External rotation versus reverse fly: the rotation family changes humeral
  rotation without horizontal abduction. A face pull combines actions and is
  outside both narrow contracts.
- Internal rotation versus fly or extension: anatomical contributors can
  produce several actions, but a role assignment never adds those actions to
  an exercise. The family signature, not muscle capability alone, owns the
  movement.

## Evidence discipline

Moment-arm and musculoskeletal-model sources establish anatomical capability;
they do not rank exercise roles by themselves. Exercise-specific EMG supports
the admitted setup and a bounded role envelope, but an excitation amplitude is
not a numeric contribution score and does not prove scapular motion.

For raises, bone-pin kinematics support normal coupled upward rotation and
posterior tilt during humeral elevation. Those data do not justify fixed
scapulohumeral ratios or interpreting scapular internal rotation as
protraction. For the cable lateral raise, the reviewed trial uses a unilateral
0-to-90-degree path and an elbow held extended; its symptom-driven path
accommodations do not admit scaption into the strict frontal contract.

Both raise contracts classify upper trapezius as a `secondary` dynamic
contributor because both declare scapular upward rotation as a prime action.
The loaded flexion/abduction model validated against EMG places superior
trapezius among the greatest activity and positive-work contributors in both
paths. A separate six-person static, unweighted comparison observed its
activity increasing gradually in both flexion and abduction; that study's
reported early path differences involved the other scapular muscles. The
shared categorical role therefore does not claim equal excitation, work, or
training emphasis across the two loaded paths: each deltoid subdivision
remains the respective family's sole primary.

For rotation, the reviewed cable and elastic studies directly examine dynamic
internal and external rotation. The external-rotator taxonomy intentionally
aggregates infraspinatus and teres minor, while subscapularis is a separate but
currently unvisualized region. The contracts therefore avoid unsupported
submuscle or visual claims.

Free-weight pullovers and straight-arm cable pulldowns share conditioned
shoulder extension but have different external resistance profiles and
exercise-specific excitation hierarchies. Their family must preserve that
equipment-conditioned primary-role difference rather than forcing a universal
“lat pullover” or “chest pullover” label.

## Deferred unlocks

`scapular-retraction` unlocks when a reviewed dynamic loaded source and an
exact fixture establish scapular translation while shoulder and elbow angles
remain controlled. Isometric-phase EMG or a row performed with a retraction
cue is insufficient.

`upright-row` unlocks when a primary dynamic kinematics source or a reviewed
measurement fixture establishes the upper-arm path, humeral rotation, endpoint,
and scapular actions for the same loaded setup. Grip-width EMG can then inform
variants, but it cannot supply those missing actions.

## Test gate

Each activated family requires an exact roster and role contract, exact fixed
classification and action signature, typed admitted-axis coverage, every rule
to have both a matching and contrasting exercise, one rejecting mutation per
encoded rule consequence, forbidden-action boundary mutations, and global
catalog-ID/name/alias/evidence validation. Batch-level tests additionally swap
the distinguishing action across every neighboring pair listed above.
